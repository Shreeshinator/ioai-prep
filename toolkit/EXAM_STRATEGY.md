# Exam strategy: 2 hours, in person, no AI

> INAIO publishes the official format (sections, marking, whether calculators are allowed) about **two weeks before the exam (around 29 Nov)**. Update this page then. Until then, assume **no calculator**.

## What the 2026 papers looked like

- **Jan 18 paper:** 70 points in 120 min.
  - Section A: ten 3-point questions (A1 reasoning/puzzles, A2 numeric modelling).
  - Section B: four 10-point blocks (B1–B4), each with a Background box and 3–7 parts.
  - Answers are MCQ (6 options), integers, or ⌊100x⌋.
- **Feb 28 alternate:** the same 14 topics in the same order, plus some **"select all that apply"** parts.
- **2025 paper** (different format): no negative marking.

## Time plan (~1.7 min per point)

1. **First 5 min:** skim the whole paper. Mark each question **Q** (quick), **M** (medium) or **H** (heavy).
2. **Pass 1 (~50 min):** all Q questions, plus the first part of each B block. Never spend more than 6 min on a 3-point question in pass 1.
3. **Pass 2 (~45 min):** the M questions and the remaining B parts.
4. **Pass 3 (~15 min):** the H questions.
5. **Last 5 min:** fill every blank, unless negative marking is announced.

## B-sections (40% of the marks)

- Read the Background box **once, slowly**, with a pen. Underline every convention: which axis is actual, strict or non-strict inequality, flip or no flip, tie-break rule, floor or round.
- Later parts reuse earlier work. Build the full table **once**, neatly, and reuse it (B1 Q7 needed the whole 3×3 matrix).

## MCQ habits

- **Eliminate with counterexamples** (M10). Two killed options are worth more than one half-proved one.
- **Use the options:** a list of numeric choices tells you the scale. An option like 25 in Pikachu hinted at a full tour, so check whether the colouring bound is reachable.
- **Plug back in:** for recurrences or equations, a candidate answer can often be checked faster than it can be derived.

## Numeric answers

- ⌊100x⌋ means the **floor** of 100x. 0.8652 → 86, not 87.
- Watch out when a value lands on an integer. P = 92.0000…1 floors to 92, but 91.9999 floors to 91. Keep exact fractions until the end.
- Sanity-check the magnitude. A probability above 1, or a precision above the base rate's possible range, means something's wrong.
